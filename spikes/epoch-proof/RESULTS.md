# Aztec on JAM, with JAM as the settlement layer: can refine check Aztec's epoch proof? (#21)

`spikes/epoch-proof/measure.sh`, 2026-10-23, Apple M-series Mac (10 cores, 16 GB).

## The question

Roadmap #21 first assumed refine would check each user's kernel proof (CHONK), about 12 billion
gas by ratio (spikes/chonk). Aztec itself never does that on Ethereum: its provers fold an
epoch's transactions into one root rollup proof, and Ethereum's Rollup contract checks only
that, with a Solidity verifier generated by Barretenberg. If JAM takes Ethereum's place
(refine checks the epoch proof, accumulate keeps the archive root and the messages), then
everything above that boundary runs as Aztec ships it: kernels, Aztec.nr, the PXE, the node,
sequencer and prover. A new Aztec release is a re-pin, plus whatever changed at the boundary.

Aztec's epoch proof (l1-contracts/script/deploy/HonkVerifier.sol, "copied from
v6@42fae4eb"; yarn-project/bb-prover's `ultra_keccak_honk` for `RootRollupArtifact`):
plain UltraHonk (no ZK), Keccak transcript, `LOG_N = 24`, 113 public inputs plus the 8
pairing-point limbs (121), 331 proof fields.

## Our verifier, ported to bb 6.0.0

Aztec v6 pins Noir 1.0.0-rc.3 and bb 6.0.0; refine's verifier (zkVerify's, at its bb 5.0.0
pull request) rejected bb 6 proofs at sumcheck. bb's own Solidity verifiers for 5.0.0 and
6.0.0 differ in exactly two things, both ported on abutlabs/ultrahonk_verifier `bb-v6`:

- **Challenges.** A challenge is the whole Keccak output mod r, no longer split into two
  127-bit halves; a round's second challenge is the hash of its first (gamma = H(beta)).
- **ROM-LogUp.** A new relation for read-only memory: one more challenge (H(eta)) and two
  subrelations (29 → 31), the later ones renumbered.

Checks: bb 6 plain and ZK proofs accepted, bb 5 proofs rejected, a flipped proof scalar or
public input rejected; the crate's fixtures regenerated with bb 6.0.0
(`scripts/generate_test_fixtures.sh`, from barretenberg's own test programs), including
`rom_read`, whose proofs fail if the ROM-LogUp relation is removed (so is the recursive
fixture: Noir's recursion uses ROM). 44 of 46 crate tests pass; the two left need an
invalid-inner fixture, which stock bb 6.0.0 refuses to emit (it verifies every proof it
makes, `bbapi_ultra_honk.cpp:151`), so they wait on a bb built without that check.

## The cost in refine, on PolkaJam

A six-validator PolkaJam v0.8.0 testnet (`polkajam-testnet`, dummy finality); the spike
service takes `[flavour][tag][vk][public inputs][proof]` from the payload, verifies in
refine, and accumulate stores the verdict. Gas and time are PolkaJam's own (JIP-3 `Refined`,
three guarantors per package); the circuits are synthetic, 113 public inputs, proved with
Aztec's flags (`-t evm-no-zk`), each checked by `bb verify` and our verifier first.

**The circuit's content matters, not only its size.** 2^23 costs 9.65M more than 2^22, where
each earlier doubling added about 1.3M, while the proof grew by exactly one round (352 bytes).
The 2^12-2^22 circuits are squarings: arithmetic gates only, so most of the verification
key's selector commitments are zero, and the verifier's curve arithmetic is cheaper on them.
The 2^23 circuit (keccakf1600) uses lookups and more gate types. Our transfer circuit, which
uses many gate types, costs 169,112,761 at 2^14 with 20 public inputs, against 155,653,349 for
the bare 2^14 circuit with 113. So the line through the squaring circuits understates a real
root rollup, which uses every gate type. Next (#23): the same sizes with a circuit that uses
every relation, to bound the real cost.

2^24 itself (Aztec's size) was not proved here: on this 16 GB machine the synthetic circuit's
witness build swapped for 50 minutes before proving began (a loop of 16.7M unrolled steps; #23).

Gas grows by about 1.3 million per doubling of the circuit (sumcheck and Gemini each gain a
round). Checking Aztec's epoch proof costs refine about a sixth of a tiny package and a
thirtieth of a full one: the 12-billion-gas wall of #21 was the per-transaction design, not
this one.

## What else the boundary takes (from reading Aztec's code, not yet built)

- **Blobs.** The root rollup's public inputs commit to the epoch's data as EIP-4844 KZG over
  BLS12-381 (a `FinalBlobAccumulator`); Ethereum checks it with the point-evaluation precompile
  (`BlobLib.sol`). JAM needs that check in refine or accumulate (a BLS12-381 pairing on the
  PVM, unmeasured), or Aztec's blob circuits change.
- **Ethereum-shaped values.** `chain_id`, `version`, 20-byte coinbase and fee recipients,
  L1↔L2 message hashes with Ethereum senders: values a JAM service can supply, with
  Ethereum's semantics.
- **The node's L1 code.** About 8–10k lines of TypeScript (archiver ingest, sequencer and
  prover publishers, epoch cache, fees, wiring). The archiver's outward interfaces and the
  epoch cache, blob client, slasher and fee builder already sit behind interfaces; the
  sequencer publisher and `RollupContract` do not, so those need seams in Aztec's code.
- **The PXE needs nothing changed**: a chain id, a version and L1 addresses from the node;
  Aztec's TXE already runs a node with no L1.
- **The Rollup contract's core** (propose, the epoch-proof check, Inbox and Outbox, about
  5k lines of Solidity besides the verifier) becomes the service's refine and accumulate;
  staking, rewards, slashing and governance are optional for a first version.
