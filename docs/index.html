Aztec on JAM

Aztec's private transactions, settled and verified on JAM in Ethereum's place. Aztec's software runs as released; JAM replaces the layer under it.

Aztec

A privacy network. Every transaction is proved on the sender's own device with a zero-knowledge proof, so the chain verifies it without learning sender, receiver or amount. Today it settles on Ethereum.

aztec.network

JAM

Polkadot's next protocol: hundreds of cores of verifiable compute. Services bring their own code; validators execute it, audit each other, and finalize the result.

Aztec on JAM

Everything Aztec takes from Ethereum — JAM provides, without an L2. We built the proof-of-concept, benchmarks, and drafted a JAM-Improvement-Proposal ( JIP ) that makes AZTEC onJAM fast.

Every number below was measured on this stack, on one Apple M-series Mac; each step names who acts and what they hand to the next. Green is Aztec's code, unchanged.

Aztec node

jam-l1 facade
our adapter process beside the node, same operator — not a JAM service, not a third party. Ethereum RPC up, JAM down

JAM chain
six lasair validators execute and audit our settlement service — a JAM service deployed on-chain, where the rollup's rules and state live
⇢
Prover cluster
one operator, three of Aztec's processes: the prover node (watches the chain for unproven epochs), the broker (queues jobs), agents (prove — 32 cores, 128 GB each by Aztec's guidance). Its proof returns through the facade ⤴ (#69)

Three operators at most: the user (browser), whoever runs the Aztec node with its facade beside it, and a prover. The chain trusts none of them — finality comes from the validators, and never waits for the prover.
